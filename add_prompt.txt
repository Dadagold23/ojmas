# OJMAS JOURNAL ENHANCEMENT PROMPT
## Module: Enhanced Archive, Article Citations, Public Advertising, and Web Push Notifications

### 1. Your role

Act as a senior Laravel developer, scholarly publishing systems architect, database engineer, frontend designer, and web-notification specialist.

Enhance the existing OJMAS Journal website at https://ojmaspublish.online with the following capabilities:

1. An enhanced journal archive and issue table of contents.
2. Complete author and co-author attribution with citation-generation functionality.
3. An advertisement and public-announcement management system.
4. A browser-based push notification system controlled by authorized administrators.
5. Administrative analytics for advertisement performance and notification delivery.

This is an enhancement of an existing application, not a new project.

### 2. Mandatory rules

- Begin with a read-only audit of the existing application.
- Verify the current Laravel version, database schema, article models, issue models, publication routes, authentication, roles, and storage configuration.
- Reuse existing functionality wherever possible.
- Preserve existing articles, PDFs, URLs, DOI records, user accounts, payment functionality, and publication metadata.
- Do not create duplicate article or issue records unnecessarily.
- Do not delete or overwrite production data.
- Do not modify existing functionality unrelated to these features.
- Use server-side authorization and secure file handling.
- Implement responsive interfaces for mobile, tablet, and desktop.
- Test each module before proceeding.
- Stop after every module and wait for my explicit approval before continuing.

If an existing component already provides a requested feature, extend it rather than duplicating it.

# MODULE 1 — ENHANCED JOURNAL ARCHIVE AND ISSUE TABLE OF CONTENTS

## 1.1 Archive page

Enhance the public archive page to display published journal volumes and issues in an organized, searchable format.

Each issue should display, where available:

- Journal title.
- Volume number.
- Issue number.
- Publication year and date.
- Issue title or theme.
- Cover image.
- Number of published articles.
- Link to view the issue.
- Optional full-issue PDF, if the journal actually provides one.

Provide filters for year, volume, and issue. Add pagination or another scalable listing method.

Only published and publicly accessible material may appear in the public archive.

## 1.2 Issue detail page

Create or enhance the public issue page with the following structure:

**Issue header**
- Volume and issue number.
- Publication date.
- Issue title or theme.
- Issue description, where available.
- Cover image, where available.
- Optional full-issue PDF download.

**Table of contents**
- Article sequence number.
- Article title.
- Full author and co-author names in the correct order.
- Article type or section.
- Abstract preview, where available.
- Page range or article identifier.
- DOI, when assigned.
- Publication date, where appropriate.
- Article detail link.
- PDF download link.

Preserve the journal's approved article ordering. Do not sort published articles arbitrarily if an editorial sequence has already been established.

Allow authorized administrators to manage the order of articles within an issue.

Do not fabricate missing metadata.

## 1.3 Individual article landing page

Every published article should have a dedicated public landing page.

Display:

- Article title.
- All authors and co-authors in the correct order.
- Author affiliations where available.
- Corresponding-author information where appropriate.
- ORCID identifiers where available.
- Journal title.
- Volume, issue, publication year, and page range or article number.
- DOI and a DOI link, if assigned.
- Abstract.
- Keywords.
- Article PDF.
- Publication licence and copyright information where recorded.
- Citation formats.
- Related issue and archive links.

Keep the existing published PDF available and preserve established article URLs wherever possible.

## 1.4 Author and co-author attribution

Use the existing author relationships where available. If the schema cannot represent multiple authors, propose a normalized author/article relationship with an explicit author-order field.

The system must support:

- Multiple authors per article.
- Correct author ordering.
- Author affiliations.
- Optional ORCID identifiers.
- Optional corresponding-author designation.
- Appropriate handling of missing metadata.
- Consistent author information across archive pages, article pages, PDFs, and citation exports.

Do not assume that the account submitting a manuscript is the only author.

## 1.5 Citation generator

Provide citation formats for published articles, including:

- APA 7.
- MLA, if supported by the implementation.
- Chicago, if supported by the implementation.
- A copyable plain-text citation.
- BibTeX export.
- RIS export.

Generate citations from verified article metadata.

For APA 7, correctly handle the available author list, publication year, article title, journal title, volume, issue, page range or article number, and DOI.

Do not invent a DOI, page range, publication year, or author name. If a required field is unavailable, report the limitation instead of generating misleading information.

Provide a Copy Citation action and downloadable citation files where appropriate.

Ensure that citations remain consistent with the official published record.

## 1.6 Search-engine and scholarly discoverability

Where supported by the current application, implement appropriate:

- Page titles and meta descriptions.
- Canonical URLs.
- Scholarly article metadata.
- Google Scholar-compatible citation metadata.
- Schema.org scholarly article structured data.
- XML sitemap entries for public articles and issues.
- Consistent DOI links and bibliographic metadata.

Do not claim that these changes guarantee indexing or inclusion in any scholarly database.

### Module 1 acceptance tests

Verify that:

- An issue displays all and only its intended published articles.
- Authors and co-authors appear in the correct order.
- Article PDFs open or download successfully.
- Citation formats use the correct metadata.
- Existing publication URLs continue working.
- Unpublished articles remain inaccessible to public visitors.
- Archive filters, pagination, and responsive layouts work.
- No existing publication records are lost.

Stop and request approval before Module 2.

# MODULE 2 — ADMINISTRATIVE ADVERTISEMENT AND ANNOUNCEMENT MANAGER

## 2.1 Objective

Create a dedicated dashboard section named:

**Advertisements & Announcements**

Access must be restricted to authorized Admin and Super Admin accounts.

The system must support both revenue-generating advertisements and official journal announcements.

## 2.2 Advertisement types

Support:

1. Image advertisements.
2. Video advertisements.
3. Audio or jingle promotions where technically appropriate.
4. Banner advertisements.
5. Calls for Papers.
6. Upcoming volume and issue announcements.
7. Publication-date announcements.
8. Conferences, workshops, and academic events.
9. Sponsored content announcements.
10. General journal news.

Do not treat every advertisement as a push notification. Administrators must be able to choose where each campaign appears.

## 2.3 Advertisement creation form

Provide the following fields where applicable:

- Campaign title.
- Campaign description.
- Campaign type.
- Advertiser or sponsor name.
- Image, video, or audio upload.
- Optional external media URL.
- Destination URL.
- Placement selection.
- Publication start date and time.
- Publication end date and time.
- Campaign status.
- Priority or display order.
- Audience selection.
- Push-notification option.
- Advertisement label.
- Optional pricing or sponsorship information.
- Optional internal notes.

Support draft, pending approval where configured, scheduled, published, paused, expired, and archived statuses.

Validate uploaded media types, sizes, dimensions, and duration as applicable. Store files securely and avoid executing uploaded content.

Do not embed arbitrary untrusted HTML or JavaScript in public advertisements.

## 2.4 Public advertisement placements

Allow administrators to configure permitted placements such as:

- Homepage banner.
- Archive page banner.
- Issue page promotional area.
- Article page banner.
- Website announcement strip.
- Dedicated Calls for Papers page.
- Sponsored-content section.

Provide mobile-responsive layouts and prevent advertisements from obscuring article content or interfering with PDF downloads.

Clearly label paid or sponsored advertisements.

Respect campaign start and end dates automatically. Expired campaigns must stop appearing without requiring manual deletion.

## 2.5 Advertisement management dashboard

Provide:

- Campaign listing.
- Search and filters.
- Create, edit, preview, publish, pause, resume, and archive actions.
- Scheduled-campaign calendar.
- Start and expiry dates.
- Campaign status indicators.
- Placement configuration.
- Media previews.
- Basic campaign performance reporting.

Implement appropriate role restrictions. Super Admin should be able to configure system-wide advertising settings, while Admin permissions should follow the existing authorization policy.

Do not grant either role unrestricted permissions merely because the interface contains administrative controls.

# MODULE 3 — PUBLIC WEB PUSH NOTIFICATION SYSTEM

## 3.1 Objective

Implement browser-based push notifications so that consenting visitors and users can receive important journal updates even when the journal website is not currently open.

Use a standards-based Web Push implementation compatible with the verified hosting environment.

Evaluate whether a suitable Laravel-compatible Web Push library is appropriate. If a third-party service is proposed, document its pricing, data handling, limitations, and configuration requirements before adoption.

Do not assume that a notification will reach every device or browser.

## 3.2 Notification categories

Support:

- New volume published.
- New issue published.
- New article published.
- Upcoming issue announcement.
- Call for Papers.
- Submission deadline reminders.
- Conference or workshop announcements.
- Sponsored announcements.
- Advertisement campaigns.
- Important journal notices.

Allow administrators to choose which categories are eligible for public push notifications.

## 3.3 Notification composer

Create a dashboard form with:

- Notification title.
- Short message.
- Notification category.
- Optional icon or image.
- Destination URL.
- Target audience.
- Publication time.
- Optional expiry time.
- Send immediately or schedule.
- Optional linked advertisement campaign.
- Draft or scheduled status.

Provide a preview before sending.

Validate message length, destination URLs, permissions, and scheduling information.

## 3.4 Public subscription

Provide a clear, optional Subscribe to Journal Updates control.

Requirements:

- Explain the notification purpose before asking for permission.
- Request browser permission only after an explicit user action.
- Support subscription management and unsubscribe actions.
- Respect browser permission denials.
- Detect unsupported browsers and provide an appropriate fallback.
- Store subscription data securely.
- Associate subscriptions with user accounts only where appropriate and permitted.
- Support anonymous visitor subscriptions without exposing personal information.
- Remove or disable invalid subscriptions after delivery failures.
- Never bypass browser notification permissions.

Implement the required service worker, HTTPS checks, Web Push subscription storage, and VAPID configuration where applicable.

Do not expose VAPID private keys or other secrets in frontend code.

For supported browsers, show notifications with a useful title, message, and destination link.

Where native push is unavailable, provide an in-site announcement centre or another suitable fallback.

## 3.5 Notification delivery

Implement reliable notification delivery using queued jobs where supported.

Include:

- Scheduled notifications.
- Delivery attempts and failure handling.
- Retry policies with sensible limits.
- Invalid-subscription cleanup.
- Duplicate-send protection.
- Delivery status reporting.
- Audit logs.
- Campaign-to-notification linkage.

Do not send duplicate notifications when a scheduled job retries.

Track the delivery results the system can actually observe. Do not report a notification as read or clicked unless the relevant event has genuinely been recorded.

## 3.6 Audience targeting

Support configurable targeting, such as:

- All consenting subscribers.
- Readers who opted into publication updates.
- Readers who opted into announcements and promotions.
- Authenticated users where appropriate.
- Other consent-based groups supported by the existing system.

Provide separate preferences for editorial or journal announcements and promotional messages where practical.

Do not send commercial advertisements to all subscribers by default. Promotional targeting must respect the user's preferences and applicable privacy requirements.

# MODULE 4 — ADVERTISEMENT REVENUE AND CAMPAIGN ANALYTICS

## 4.1 Objective

Provide a basic advertising-management and performance-reporting system without assuming that the journal already has a payment gateway for advertisers.

Record where applicable:

- Campaign impressions.
- Advertisement clicks.
- Click-through rate.
- Campaign start and expiry.
- Advertiser or sponsor.
- Campaign placement.
- Campaign status.
- Agreed advertising price, if recorded.
- Payment status, if supported by an approved payment workflow.

Define how impressions and clicks are counted. Prevent obvious duplicate or automated event inflation where practical.

Distinguish between:

- Agreed advertising revenue.
- Invoiced revenue.
- Paid revenue.
- Estimated campaign performance.

Do not label an unpaid invoice as collected revenue.

## 4.2 Revenue controls

Provide optional fields for:

- Advertiser details.
- Campaign quotation or agreement reference.
- Price and currency.
- Invoice reference.
- Payment status.
- Payment date.
- Internal notes.

Use the existing approved payment architecture if online advertiser payments are later authorized.

Do not modify the journal's existing Paystack payment processing without explicit approval.

Do not invent payment records or automatically charge advertisers.

## 4.3 Reports

Provide dashboard summaries and date-filtered reports for:

- Active advertisements.
- Scheduled campaigns.
- Expired campaigns.
- Impressions and clicks.
- Campaign performance.
- Recorded advertising income.
- Outstanding recorded invoices, if implemented.
- Notification delivery results.

Restrict financial reports to authorized roles.

# MODULE 5 — AUTOMATED ISSUE AND ANNOUNCEMENT SCHEDULING

Implement scheduling tools that allow administrators to prepare upcoming issue announcements in advance.

For each future volume or issue, allow the authorized user to enter:

- Volume number.
- Issue number.
- Planned publication date.
- Call-for-Papers opening date.
- Submission deadline.
- Announcement publication date.
- Related landing page.
- Optional banner or promotional media.
- Optional notification campaign.

The system may automatically prepare or publish approved announcements at configured times.

However, a scheduled announcement must not automatically create a published issue or publish unpublished articles.

If the issue is delayed, administrators must be able to change the schedule, pause the announcement, or cancel queued notifications.

Use the existing issue records where possible. Do not create duplicate issue entries.

Provide a calendar or schedule view for upcoming publication dates and campaigns.
